# Third review pass, 1 November 2026

The first two passes (`audit_2026-10-11.md`, `resolutions_2026-10-11.md`,
`audit_pass2_2026-10-12.md`) worked from the model's structure and then from
every number in the paper. This pass started from a different place: it read the
paper as a hostile reviewer would, looking for the sentence that would be
attacked first, and then asked whether the attack would succeed.

Six things came out of it. One was a genuinely wrong label on the headline
number. Two were claims the paper had already retreated from in the body but not
in the abstract. Two became new analysis, and in both cases the analysis came
back *more* favourable to the paper than the limitation it was testing. The last
was a style regression introduced by this pass itself and then fixed.

---

## A. The headline interval was mislabelled

**A1. "95% interval across all parameter uncertainty" was wrong twice.**
`07_sensitivity.py` draws all sixteen inputs uniformly and independently over
ranges chosen by judgement. The 2.5-to-97.5 percentile spread of the output is
therefore a sensitivity range conditional on those ranges: there is no
likelihood and no prior, so it is neither a confidence interval nor a credible
one. The result files always said `p2.5` and `p97.5`, which was honest; the
prose upgraded them. And it was not "all parameter uncertainty", since the
delivery and translation constants are not in that ensemble at all. Audit A6
established the second point and Methods 2.1 was narrowed accordingly, but the
abstract and section 3.13 kept the wide claim.

Section 3.13 was retitled and rewritten to state what the ensemble is, to say
plainly what it is not, and to lead with the claim it actually supports, which
is that the benefit is positive in every sample rather than that it lies in any
particular interval. Added as Limitations 22 and 23.

## B. Two claims the abstract had not retreated from

**B1. The abstract still asserted first-order MSH3 dependence.** Audit A3 forced
section 3.2 to narrow its claim from "first-order" to "not a cooperative
assembly", and section 3.2 says so carefully, including that at achievable
knockdown levels k = 1 and k = 1.5 differ by a few points. The abstract still
said partial lowering "buys suppression in direct proportion, with no
cooperative threshold and no cooperative bonus", which is the k = 1 claim. The
section heading 3.2 also still read "is first-order in MSH3". Both corrected,
along with the same assertion in the introduction's contribution list and in the
falsifiable-predictions list.

**B2. R was quoted without its feasible range, and the range is six-fold.**
`profile_intervals.csv` gives R a 10%-tolerance range of 3.679 to 22.89. L_sw
had its interval quoted everywhere; R never did, although both are fitted and
sit in the same table. A reviewer who finds a six-fold interval behind a headline
number after reading the abstract discounts everything else. Now quoted in the
abstract, in section 3.1 where 17.7 first appears, and in the parameter table,
with the statement that what the anchors establish is an order of magnitude
rather than a value.

## C. Two limitations that turned into results

**C1. The switch width was promoted from caveat to finding. (presentation)**
`wsw_profile.csv` has shown since audit A4 that an exact fit to the anchors
exists only for widths between 4.0 and 7.5 repeats. That was reported as a
conditionality in section 3.1 and as Limitation 7. It is better than that: it
means the published dwell times *require* a sharp transition, because a
transition spread over a dozen repeats cannot give twelve years from 60 to 80
and four years from 80 to 150 at once. Section 3.1 now reports the admissible
window as a result, with the joint consequence that the midpoint lies between
76.4 and 85.3 repeats across the widths that fit. Limitation 7 was rewritten to
say that the sharpness is now a result and only the midpoint stays conditional.
The falsifiable-predictions list now carries the quantitative form: the rise
should be complete within about four to eight repeats.

**C2. The exposure-response curve is better constrained than its construction
suggests. (new analysis, `19_exposure_response_band.py`, section 3.14)**
Limitation 5 states that the Emax curve has two parameters on two points, zero
degrees of freedom, and is extrapolated. True, and it carries the whole delivery
and dosing analysis. What was never asked is how much that costs. Profiling it
the way `10_profile.py` profiles the rate law, by fixing the Hill coefficient
and refitting EC50 through the two points, answers it: at the same 10% tolerance
used for the rate law the admissible Hill window is 1.05 to 1.35, and EC50 moves
only from 9.85 to 10.26 nmol g-1. The reason is geometric, not luck: the two
doses sit at 7.5 and 25 nmol g-1, straddling the fitted EC50, so between them
they pin both position and steepness. A Hill coefficient of 0.5 misses a measured
point by 41% and one of 3.0 by 83%.

Propagated, the headline requirement of 25 nmol g-1 becomes 23.1 to 28.1, and
7 mg becomes 6.5 to 7.8 mg. The reported 18-to-36-fold systemic shortfall becomes
17 to 40-fold, since the required systemic dose is proportional to the required
exposure. The band widens with extrapolation distance exactly as it should:
-3 to +1% at 50% knockdown, -7 to +12% at 75%, and -15 to +29% at 90%.

So the limitation is real but narrower than stated. It was rewritten to locate
itself where it bites, which is the dosing grid of section 3.11 reaching
388 nmol g-1, fifteen times the highest calibration point. Those rows now carry
an explicit instruction to read them as the shape of the half-life-to-interval
trade-off rather than as calibrated exposures.

**C3. Correlated calibration anchors. (new analysis,
`18_anchor_correlation.py`, section 3.15)**
Nobody had raised this and it is the first thing a modelling reviewer would ask.
The three dwell-time anchors are sampled independently although all three come
from one single-cell study on one post-mortem cohort, so a systematic error in
that study's clock would move all three together in a way independent sampling
does not allow. The whole global analysis was repeated with those three coupled
through a Gaussian copula at latent correlations of 0, 0.5 and 0.9. A copula
rather than a shared multiplicative factor, so that every anchor keeps exactly
the uniform marginal it has in the independent design and only the dependence
changes.

The result splits cleanly. Correlation widens the spread of *absolute* loss by a
third, with the central 95% range for untreated loss at 70 growing from 36.9 to
48.7 percentage points. It leaves the *benefit* alone: median 11.48, 11.43,
11.45 across the three correlations, no trend in width, and positive in all 160
samples at every correlation. The benefit is a difference between two arms
simulated with the same sampled parameters and the same seeds, so a common-mode
clock error largely cancels in the difference while surviving in each arm.

One finding here cuts against the paper and is reported as such. The rho = 0 arm
is the same model and ranges as section 3.13, differing only in sampler. Its
median agrees to two decimal places, 11.48 in both, which validates the
implementation. But its central 95% range is 6.4 to 21.2 points against 7.4 to
17.5 from the Latin hypercube, about 45% wider, because Latin hypercube
stratification reduces ensemble variance. Part of the narrowness of the reported
interval was a property of the sampler rather than of the model. That is
Limitation 22, and the wider figure is the more conservative one.

## D. Smaller corrections

- **D1.** Sections 3.3 and 3.5 quote mean loss at CAP 600 as 43.5% and 39.8%
  within two pages. Both are right: 43.5% averages the six inherited lengths of
  `cap600_check.csv`, 40 to 50 repeats, where the 40-repeat case contributes
  65.1% and lifts the mean; 39.8% averages the three lengths carried through the
  estimator comparison in `17_estimator.py`, which are 42, 46 and 50. Section
  3.5 now labels both rather than leaving a reader to find the discrepancy.
- **D2.** `population_validation.csv` has six rows against the paper's five
  held-out observations, because the terminal-length comparison is written out
  as a median and a 97.5th percentile. Stated in section 3.3.
- **D3.** The abstract's Results paragraph ran 590 words and roughly thirty
  numbers, several conditional, before reaching the finding that matters. Cut to
  423 words and restructured so the argument is followable: switch-like and
  sharp, non-cooperative in MSH3, therefore disease-modifying and powerable,
  but delivery falls short. Numbers that only exist to be complete were left to
  the results tables, which is where they are written by code anyway.
- **D4.** The parameter table now carries feasible ranges beside the fitted
  point values for R, L_sw and w_sw, and the zero-degrees-of-freedom note beside
  EC50 and the Hill coefficient.
- **D5.** Section 6 now cites `review/` as part of the method rather than
  leaving it in the repository unmentioned, and says what the passes caught. For
  a sole-authored computational paper with no primary data, a documented
  adversarial re-reading is the substitute for the co-author who would otherwise
  catch those things.

## E. A regression introduced by this pass, and fixed

**E1.** The new sections used em dashes and the Greek letter rho. Audit pass 2
finding 9 had removed every em dash from the IEEE builds, so reintroducing them
was a style regression against a resolved issue; `15_build_ieee.py` also warned
that 16 non-ASCII characters survived translation, because rho was missing from
its substitution table. All em dashes removed from `paper.md`, and rho added to
`SINGLE` in `15_build_ieee.py` so the character translates in future. The IEEE
build now reports no remaining non-ASCII characters.

## Verification

- A clean re-run of `01_calibrate.py` reproduces every committed result table
  byte for byte, which is what licenses trusting the two new analyses.
- After adding the new analyses, nothing in `results/` changed. The only
  differences are three new files: `anchor_correlation.csv`,
  `exposure_response_band.csv` and `hill_profile.csv`. No pre-existing result
  was perturbed.
- Every number introduced into the paper by this pass was traced back to the
  result file that produced it.
- `09_build_documents.py` and `16_build_ieee_docx.py` rebuild cleanly, both
  picking up seventeen figures. `15_build_ieee.py` regenerates the IEEE LaTeX
  source cleanly and fails only at the `pdflatex` call, because no TeX
  distribution is installed in the environment this pass ran in. That is an
  environment gap, not a code fault: the source it produces is complete and
  includes both new figures.

## What did not change

The model, the calibration, the fitted parameters and every conclusion. Of the
six findings, one was a wrong label, two were stale claims in the abstract that
the body had already corrected, two were new analyses that narrowed stated
limitations rather than widening them, and one was self-inflicted. The rate law,
the switch position, the therapeutic benefit and the delivery arithmetic are
where they were.

## What is still not fixed, and why

- The exposure-response curve still has two points behind it, and the dosing
  grid still runs far outside them. Section 3.14 bounds the part that can be
  bounded; the rest needs more published doses.
- Delivery uncertainty and expansion uncertainty are still never propagated
  together. Each has its own error budget and the two are never combined, which
  is Limitation 23.
- The MSH3 binding order still rests on two measurements from a regression with
  R-squared 0.65. A fine dose titration in patient-derived striatal neurons
  remains the single most informative experiment this model points to.
- Safety is still not modelled, and no amount of efficacy modelling substitutes
  for measuring it.
