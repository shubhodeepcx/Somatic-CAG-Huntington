# Second review pass, 12 October 2026

The first pass (`audit_2026-10-11.md`, `resolutions_2026-10-11.md`) worked
from the structure of the model outwards. This pass did the opposite: every
number quoted in the paper was traced back to the result table it came from,
and every script was re-read for constants that had been copied rather than
read. Nine things turned up. Two of them were wrong numbers in the paper, one
was a wrong mechanism, and the rest were missing disclosures.

## Findings and what was done

**1. The depth-scaling figures in section 3.8 did not come from the code.**
The paper said a human putaminal depth of 14.8 mm and a 3.4-fold further
attenuation. Running `05_delivery.py` gives 24.4 mm under cube-root scaling
and a 39.8-fold penalty; 14.8 corresponds to no constant anywhere in the
script. The cause was that the depth estimate had been edited in prose
without being recomputed. The point estimate was the wrong fix in any case,
because the answer swings by more than an order of magnitude on an assumed
number. `05_delivery.py` now sweeps four depth scenarios into
`results/depth_attenuation.csv` (12, 16 and 20 mm anatomical, plus 24.4 mm
from cube-root scaling, giving 1.7, 4.6, 12.9 and 39.8-fold), section 3.8
reports the range and names 16 mm as the scenario carried forward, section
2.9 says the same, and the abstract quotes the range.

**2. Section 3.12 had the enrolment-age effect backwards.** The paper said
enrolling at 50 rather than 40 "slightly reduces the required sample size,
from 133 to 165", which is an increase, and attributed it to older
participants losing neurons faster. `trial_power.csv` shows the opposite:
n rises 417 to 523 at two years and 133 to 165 at three, because the effect
is smaller in the older cohort (0.89 against 1.02 points), and the ordering
reverses only at five years (45 against 47) because the between-subject
spread from the CAG distribution falls from 1.33 to 0.72 points. Rewritten
with the real numbers and the real mechanism, and the "easier to power"
caution scoped to the five-year case where it holds.

**3. The Emax constants were hard-coded in two places.** `08_translation.py`
and `13_dosing.py` both carried `EC50, HILL = 10.12, 1.215` as literals,
fitted in `05_delivery.py`. Re-running the fit would have left them stale
silently. Both now read `results/delivery_constants.csv`. Re-running both
reproduces every figure the paper quotes, so nothing downstream moves.

**4. The power calculation omitted modifier-genotype variance.** Its only
between-subject terms were the enrolled CAG spread and imaging noise, while
section 3.7 shows modifiers moving age at 25% loss by more years than the
CAG range does. Since that variance enters the sample-size formula as an
additive term in the variance, it can be priced without re-simulating.
`14_trial_power.py` now writes `results/modifier_variance_power.csv`: an
extra between-subject SD of 1 point raises the achievable-dose arm from 133
to 148 per arm, 2 points to 193. Reported in section 3.12 and as a new
limitation.

**5. Section 3.11 overstated the exactness of the time-average shortcut.**
Methods 2.10 already carried the conditionality (the data constrain the
MSH3 order to the neighbourhood of one, not to one precisely), but the
Results section asserted exactness flatly. Now cross-references 2.10.

**6. The dosing model has no protein-turnover lag.** It converts a drug
exposure waveform straight into an MSH3 waveform. Real protein recovery lags
the drug by its own turnover, which smooths the troughs of an intermittent
schedule, so the omission makes infrequent dosing look worse than it is.
Added as a limitation with the direction stated.

**7. The transcytosis optimum is a two-point fit.** Two free constants,
two usable monovalent affinities, zero degrees of freedom; the interior
optimum follows from the functional form rather than from the data. The same
disclosure the Emax fit already carried now appears for this fit, in section
3.9 and in the limitations.

**8. Four small inconsistencies in quoted numbers.** The baseline age at
25% loss in section 3.7 was 51.7 against 51.8 from `12_modifiers.py`; the
"benefit roughly halves per decade" claim in section 4.2 does not match the
grid (17.9, 13.7, 9.7, 7.3 points preserved at 70 for start ages 20, 30, 40,
50, while the delay to 25% loss falls much faster, 12.6, 8.2, 3.8, 0.0); the
7 mg striatal dose was quoted without its 14 kDa assumption; and sections
3.10 and 3.11 quote 47.3% and 48.3% untreated loss at 70 for the same
carrier, which is independent seeding and is now labelled as such, as the
8.2-against-8.6 case in section 3.10 already was. All four corrected.

**9. Remaining em dashes in the IEEE builds.** The abstract and index-terms
run-in heads were still em-dashed, because IEEEtran hard-codes the dash in
the class and the python-docx builder had it as a literal. Both now use a
colon: `15_build_ieee.py` redefines the two class macros in the preamble,
keeping the class default layout otherwise, and `16_build_ieee_docx.py`
writes the colon directly. The only remaining en dash in either output is a
page range in a bibliography entry, which is correct typography.

## What did not change

The model, the calibration and every conclusion. Of the nine findings, three
were disclosures of assumptions already visible in the code, four were
wrong or under-specified numbers in the prose, and two were code hygiene.
None of them touched the rate law, the fitted parameters or the therapeutic
result. Re-running every untouched script reproduces its result table
byte-for-byte, including `trial_power.csv` after the modifier-variance
addition.

## One thing the first pass got unfair

`resolutions_2026-10-11.md` recorded the original audit as wrong about the
157 nmol/g exposure ceiling. It was not: 157 is the steady-state peak for
the 75%-knockdown schedule the paper headlines, and 388 is the maximum
across the whole grid including the 90% target. Both are right about
different subsets. That entry has been corrected.
